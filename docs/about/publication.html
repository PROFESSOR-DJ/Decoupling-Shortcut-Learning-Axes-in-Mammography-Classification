    Publication

    Paper#

    Decoupling Shortcut Learning Axes in Mammography Classification Dhiraaj K V and Raghesh Krishnan K International Conference on Machine Learning and Data Engineering (ICMLDE 2026), to appear in Procedia Computer Science (Elsevier).

    Topics: shortcut learning · mammography · breast density · explainable AI · worst-group robustness · distance correlation

    Summary#

    AI models that look for cancer in mammograms can learn to rely on breast density instead of the lesion. This work splits that shortcut into two parts: density hidden in the model's features, and attention that drifts outside the breast. A density–cancer link (r ≈ 0.6) is added to the CBIS-DDSM training data only, and four fixes are compared with a baseline over 13 random seeds.

    • Removing density from the features with distance correlation shrinks the group AUROC gap from 0.357 to 0.083, but the attention stays outside the breast.
    • Penalizing off-breast attention with CAM regularization cuts it from 0.557 to 0.018, but the gap stays almost the same.
    • Both together fix both problems (gap 0.065, off-breast attention 0.021) for about two AUROC points.

    This split shows up again for a weaker shortcut, for DenseNet-121 and ResNet-50, for the clinical density grouping, over many penalty weights, and on 192 INbreast digital mammograms. A carefully tuned adversary can also narrow the gap, but none of the feature or reweighting methods repairs the attention. Each problem needs its own fix.
