    Evaluation

    Group gap (main measure)#

    The test images are split into two groups:

    • Fits the shortcut ("aligned"): a dense cancer, or a non-dense benign case.
    • Breaks the shortcut ("conflicting"): a non-dense cancer, or a dense benign case.

    A model that relies on density does well on the first group and badly on the second. The group gap is the difference in AUROC between them:

    \[ \Delta_{\text{gap}} = \mathrm{AUROC}_a - \mathrm{AUROC}_c \]

    The two group scores are listed on their own as well, since the gap can shrink because one group improves or because the other gets worse.

    Off-breast attention (SAS)#

    The spurious attribution score is the share of the model's heat map that falls outside the breast mask, averaged over the test images. \(M_i\) is 1 inside the breast and 0 outside, and \(\mathrm{CAM}_i\) is the heat-map value at pixel \(i\):

    \[ \mathrm{SAS} = \frac{\sum_i \mathrm{CAM}_i \,(1 - M_i)}{\sum_i \mathrm{CAM}_i} \]

    Note

    SAS tells you where the model looks. It does not tell you how much it relies on density, since density itself is inside the breast.

    Density probe#

    A new, simple classifier (logistic regression) is trained on the model's frozen features to guess density, repeated over 20 random resamples. High accuracy means density is still easy to read from the features. Because always guessing "dense" already gives 0.848, balanced accuracy and AUROC of the probe are reported too.

    Calibration and threshold#

    • ECE (10 bins) and the Brier score show how well the predicted probabilities match reality.
    • The best decision threshold is chosen on validation data with Youden's J.
    • Sensitivity and specificity are reported at 0.5 and at that threshold, for all test images and for the group that breaks the shortcut.

    Statistics#

    • Every main result is repeated with 13 random seeds (42–54). Each method is compared with the baseline seed by seed, giving a mean difference, a 95% bootstrap confidence interval (5,000 resamples) and a Wilcoxon signed-rank p-value.
    • With 13 paired runs that all move the same way, the smallest possible p-value is \(2 \times 0.5^{13} \approx 2.44 \times 10^{-4}\). Several results hit this floor.
    • There are 16 comparisons (4 methods × 4 measures). The group gap is the main one. 12 are significant at 0.05, 12 stay significant after Benjamini–Hochberg correction, and 8 after the stricter Bonferroni correction. The gap results for distance correlation and both fixes survive even Bonferroni.
    • The extra studies use 5 seeds (42–46). Five runs can never give a Wilcoxon p-value below 0.0625, so for these the confidence interval and the number of runs pointing the same way carry the evidence.

    Training settings#

    Setting Value
    Network ResNet-18 (ImageNet), pooled features + one linear layer
    Input Grayscale 224 × 224, three channels, left–right flips
    Optimizer Adam, learning rate 10⁻⁴, batch size 32
    Training length Up to 20 epochs; stop early on validation AUROC (patience 5), only after the 8-epoch warm-up
    Data split 70% train and validation, 30% test, grouped by patient; 20% of the first part used for validation
    Penalty weights Distance correlation 8; CAM 2; GRL λ up to 5, weight 2
    Breast mask Otsu threshold after a 5 × 5 blur, largest region kept
    Density probe Logistic regression on a 70/30 split of test features, 20 resamples
    Seeds 13 (42–54); 5 (42–46) for the extra studies
    Hardware NVIDIA RTX 4060 Laptop GPU, PyTorch 2.5.1

    Running the main comparison a second time gave the same per-run numbers, to four decimal places, for the baseline, distance correlation, CAM regularization and both fixes.
