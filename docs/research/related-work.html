    Research

    Related work

    Earlier work falls into a few families. The table lists the main studies in each family and what they do.

    Family What it does
    Adversarial removal (Ganin & Lempitsky 2015; Elazar & Goldberg 2018; Kumar et al. 2022) Trains a second classifier to find the unwanted attribute and pushes the main model to hide it. The attribute can often still be recovered afterwards.
    Unstable debiasing (Amir et al. 2021; Orgad & Belinkov 2023; Ganesh et al. 2023, 2024; Yata et al. 2026) Shows that fairness results change a lot with the random seed and data split, and that adversarial training is unstable.
    Dependence penalties (Székely et al. 2007; Zhen et al. 2022; Guo et al. 2024; Kavak et al. 2025; Díaz Berenguer et al. 2024; Müller et al. 2024; Belghazi et al. 2018; Cheng et al. 2020) Adds a penalty, such as distance correlation or mutual information, when the features depend on the unwanted attribute. Müller et al. (2024) compared these on medical images.
    Group-robust training (Sagawa et al. 2020; Liu et al. 2021) Gives more weight to the worst-off group (Group DRO) or to hard examples (JTT).
    Heat-map supervision (Selvaraju et al. 2017; Zhou et al. 2016; Mahapatra et al. 2022; Xu et al. 2021; Miguel et al. 2025; Uddin et al. 2025; Viviano et al. 2021) Uses CAM or Grad-CAM maps as a training signal. Better maps do not always mean better generalization.
    Shortcuts in radiology (Tsue et al. 2023; Zepeda-Reyes et al. 2026; Woo et al. 2025; Khara et al. 2024; Banerjee et al. 2023; Brown et al. 2023) Documents hidden cues and group gaps in medical imaging models.
    Breast cancer detection (Varun et al. 2024; Pattanayak et al. 2025; Anitha & Datta 2023; Kanimozhi et al. 2025) Improves detection and segmentation accuracy.

    How this project uses them#

    • It tests gradient reversal with its usual settings and with seven other settings.
    • It asks the question Müller et al. (2024) did not: does a feature-level fix also fix the heat maps?
    • It measures both problems for every method, because better heat maps may not mean a fairer model.
    • It uses the worst-group test of Sagawa et al. (2020), with Group DRO and JTT as comparison methods.

    The full list of papers is on the References page.
