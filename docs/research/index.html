    Research

    Research overview

    The question#

    If a mammography model leans on breast density to predict cancer, can one change to training fix it?

    Short answer: no. The shortcut causes two different problems, and fixing one leaves the other in place.

    What this work adds#

    • Two problems, two fixes. Removing density from the features fixes the group gap but not the heat maps. Penalizing off-breast attention fixes the heat maps but not the gap. This holds across many settings and a second dataset.
    • The combined fix works. Using both penalties together fixes both problems for a small loss in accuracy. Its effect on calibration and on the best decision threshold is also measured.
    • Adversarial training is fragile. A common adversarial method fails badly with default settings but works well once tuned. Even then, no adversarial, mutual-information or group-reweighting method tested here fixes the heat maps.
