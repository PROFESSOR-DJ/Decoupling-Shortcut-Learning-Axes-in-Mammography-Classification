    Results

    Discussion

    Two separate problems#

    The results back up a warning from Viviano et al. (2021): a model can learn a shortcut in its features and point its attention in the wrong place, and these are mostly independent. Distance correlation fixes the first and not the second. CAM regularization does the reverse. This stays true across shortcut strengths, backbones, density definitions and penalty weights.

    Why this matters#

    If only the feature problem is fixed, the model still produces heat maps that point outside the breast. A radiologist looking at those maps would notice straight away, yet the density probe alone would say everything is fine.

    Default gradient reversal failed, much as Elazar and Goldberg (2018) found for adversarial removal. Here the failure came mainly from resetting the extra head during training, and a tuned GRL worked about as well as distance correlation. So adversarial training is not worse in principle. It needs careful tuning, and like every other feature-level method tested, it does not fix the heat maps.

    Film versus digital mammograms#

    CBIS-DDSM images are scanned from film. Clinics now mostly use digital machines, which give sharper contrast and performed better on dense breasts in a large trial (Pisano et al., 2005). Neither fix cares how an image was made: one looks at the link between features and density, the other at heat-map mass outside the breast mask. On the digital INbreast images, the gap reduction and the CAM effect both appeared again, although distance correlation also lowered SAS there. This supports, but does not prove, that the findings carry over to digital mammography.

    Conclusion#

    One fix is not enough. A breast-density shortcut causes two mostly separate problems:

    • Distance correlation took the group gap from 0.357 to 0.083 but left the heat maps outside the breast.
    • CAM regularization took off-breast attention from 0.557 to 0.018 but hardly changed the gap.
    • Both together reached a gap of 0.065 and SAS of 0.021, for about two AUROC points.

    The same picture appeared with a weak shortcut, with two other backbones, with the clinical density split and over a wide spread of weights, and INbreast leaned the same way. A well-set adversary can close the gap too, yet no feature or reweighting method moved the heat maps back onto the breast.

    Takeaway

    Test a mammography model for shortcuts twice: once for what its features contain, and once for where it looks. A pass on one check tells you almost nothing about the other. With both fixes, the model treats dense and fatty breasts more evenly and its heat maps can be trusted, for a small and measured price. Choose its cut-off on validation data.
