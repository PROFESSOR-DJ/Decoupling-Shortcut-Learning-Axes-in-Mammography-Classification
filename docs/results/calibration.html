    Calibration and mask checks

    Calibration and decision threshold#

    Average over 13 runs from a separate repeat of the main experiment. "Thr." is the decision threshold that maximizes Youden's J on validation data. Each pair is sensitivity / specificity, for all test images and for the group that breaks the shortcut.

    Method ECE Brier Thr. All @ 0.5 All @ Thr. Breaks shortcut @ 0.5 Breaks shortcut @ Thr.
    Baseline 0.389 0.385 0.895 0.945 / 0.256 0.744 / 0.564 0.854 / 0.193 0.576 / 0.511
    CAM regularization 0.394 0.389 0.925 0.954 / 0.250 0.725 / 0.562 0.884 / 0.183 0.598 / 0.506
    Distance correlation 0.347 0.346 0.774 0.990 / 0.074 0.729 / 0.571 0.989 / 0.070 0.745 / 0.549
    Both fixes 0.338 0.337 0.755 0.988 / 0.069 0.735 / 0.537 0.980 / 0.062 0.746 / 0.516
    • CAM regularization does not shift calibration. None of its 17 calibration and threshold measures differs clearly from the baseline (for example, ECE +0.005 [−0.028, +0.043]; threshold +0.029 [−0.028, +0.086]).
    • ECE is high for every method (0.34–0.39). The training data is 78.6% cancers, but the test data is 40.8%.
    • Distance correlation and both fixes move the best cut-off down by 0.12–0.14; left at 0.5, they flag almost everything (specificity near 0.07). Once the cut-off comes from validation data, they perform like the baseline and find more of the hard cancers (sensitivity 0.75 compared with 0.58).
    • On INbreast, where validation sets hold just 20–32 images, CAM regularization moved the cut-off down (−0.129 [−0.200, −0.058]) and made calibration worse on the hard group (ECE +0.104 [+0.046, +0.172]). Nothing like this appears on CBIS-DDSM.

    In practice

    Pick the decision threshold on validation data. At a fixed 0.5 cut-off, the distance-correlation models look very unspecific; at the validation threshold they do as well as the baseline and catch more of the hard cancers.

    Checking the breast mask#

    Because the attention penalty and SAS both rely on the automatic breast outline, the outline itself was tested twice.

    1. Automatic checks on all 3,103 masks. Quick tests on size, number of pieces, contrast and top-to-bottom coverage passed 90.5% of the masks without a flag. Compared with masks drawn at full resolution, the small 224 × 224 masks overlap very well (average Dice 0.970 over 40 images); a few cut off some of the pale fatty border.

    2. Shrinking and growing the mask. SAS was recomputed with the mask grown and shrunk by 3 and 6 pixels. The baseline's SAS moves between 0.487 and 0.627, but every method's change from the baseline keeps the same direction and stays clear of zero:

    Method Change in SAS across mask sizes
    CAM regularization (CBIS-DDSM) −0.480 to −0.583
    Both fixes (CBIS-DDSM) −0.477 to −0.580
    Distance correlation (CBIS-DDSM) +0.081 to +0.100
    CAM regularization (INbreast) −0.414 to −0.459

    Note

    There are no expert-drawn breast outlines to compare against. These checks show that SAS does not depend on the exact mask edge; they do not prove the mask is perfect.
