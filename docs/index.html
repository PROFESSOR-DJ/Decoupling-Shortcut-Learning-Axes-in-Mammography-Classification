    ICMLDE 2026 · CBIS-DDSM · INbreast

    One shortcut, two failures, two fixes.

    A mammography model can learn to guess cancer from how dense the breast is instead of from the lesion. This project shows that the shortcut damages the model in two separate ways, and that each one needs its own fix.

    A known density–cancer link was added to the training data only, five training methods were compared over 13 runs, and every model was checked for both problems.

    Two problems, two fixes#

    Feature problem

    Density hides in the features

    What goes wrong
    The model keeps density inside its learned features and uses it to predict cancer, so it fails on patients who break the pattern.
    Measured by
    Group AUROC gap and a density probe
    Fix
    A distance-correlation penalty between features and density
    Attention problem

    The heat map leaves the breast

    What goes wrong
    Grad-CAM heat maps light up the background and image edges instead of breast tissue.
    Measured by
    SAS: the share of the heat map outside the breast
    Fix
    A penalty on any attention outside the breast mask

    Finding: each fix solves only its own problem. Used together, they solve both, and this holds with weaker shortcuts, other backbones, another density definition, many penalty weights and digital mammograms.
